§ 02  About

Learning by rewriting the tools I use.

NOTE A1337 has no teachers and no lectures. You get a subject PDF, a deadline, and three peers who will try to break your code at evaluation.

NOTE BNorminette-compliant since 2025. Functions under 25 lines, which is a discipline and occasionally a hostage situation.

Before 1337, I went through the ALX Software Engineering program, where I wrote my first Flask app, my first MySQL schema, and my first embarrassing commit message. It taught me to ship. 1337 taught me to understand what I was shipping.

The projects I care about most are the ones that forced me to reimplement something I’d taken for granted: a shell, so I’d understand fork and file descriptors; an IRC server, so I’d understand why everyone says “just use non-blocking I/O”; a Docker stack built without a single pre-made image.2

Lately I’ve been pulling that back up the stack — small web services, a URL shortener, a team project called HackSpace — and finding that the boring infrastructure parts are the parts I enjoy.

A program that works is a draft. A program that survives evaluation is the finished piece.
